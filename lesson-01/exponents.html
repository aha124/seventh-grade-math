Lesson 1  /  Exponents

Change the two numbers and watch the tiles. The exponent is a count of tiles, nothing else.

Base

Exponent
2

    Teacher notes

    Run it in this order

    Start at 2 squared and leave it there a moment. Two tiles, a 2 by 2 square of dots, four dots. Then walk the exponent up one at a time with the base left alone, so she sees exactly one tile appear per tap. Change the base only after that lands.

    When you reach 5 cubed, stop before tapping anything. Ask her what she thinks it will come to and write the guess down, then tap through the multiply steps together. The guess is the point. Being wrong about it is useful.

    Then push on to the fourth and the fifth, so she meets the fact that the picture runs out before the arithmetic does.

    The trap

    The mistake to expect is reading 4 cubed as 4 times 3, which gives 12. It is a reasonable guess, since the 3 is sitting right there looking like a factor.

    Catch it by counting tiles out loud. Three tiles, each one a 4. The 3 never appears inside the multiplication at all, and that is the whole idea. If she says 12, do not correct it straight away. Ask her to point at where the 3 would go in 4 times 4 times 4. There is nowhere to put it, and she will usually get there herself.

    A useful follow-up: ask what 4 to the first power should be. If the exponent were a multiplier the answer would be 4, and it is, so the trap survives. Then ask about 4 squared. A multiplier says 8; the tiles say 16. That gap is the argument.

    The nine to memorize

    These come up constantly and are worth knowing cold rather than working out each time.

    • 2² = 4    3² = 9    4² = 16    5² = 25    10² = 100
    • 2³ = 8    3³ = 27    5³ = 125    10³ = 1000

    Build each one on this page first, so the number has a shape attached before it becomes a fact to recall. The speed round on the practice page drills these same nine.

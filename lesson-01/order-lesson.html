One Step at a Time

Lesson 1  /  Order of operations

Do not work it out in your head. Tap the one operation you would do next, and only that one. It collapses into its value and the expression gets shorter.

The four ranks
  1. 1Parentheseswhatever is inside them, before anything outside
  2. 2Exponentsthe powers you built yesterday
  3. 3Multiply and divideleft to right, whichever one comes first
  4. 4Add and subtractleft to right, whichever one comes first

Four ranks, not six separate steps. Multiplying and dividing share rank 3, so neither one outranks the other and the one further left goes first. Adding and subtracting share rank 4 the same way.

Teacher notes

Run all three, in this order

Example 1 is the plain sweep through all four ranks. Let her tap the parentheses first without saying anything, then stop at 10 ÷ 5 × 2 at the end and ask which of those two goes first. That is the whole point of the example. If she says the multiplication because it feels bigger, do not correct her yet, let her tap it and read the message.

Example 2 is here for one reason: 7(3). Ask her what it means before she taps anything. Most seventh graders read it as a parentheses step because there are brackets on the page. It is multiplication with the times sign left out, and it waits its turn at rank 3 like any other multiplication. The (7 − 2) at the far right is the real parentheses step, and it goes first even though it is last in line.

Example 3 is the hardest of the three because the exponent is buried at the end, inside 2 · 3². She has to see the exponent before the multiplication that is sitting right next to it. The middle dot is another way to write times, worth pointing out once.

Do all three in one sitting if she has the patience. If not, one and two together, then three the next day, because example three leans on both of the first two.

The four wrong taps and what each one tells you

  • Parentheses first, always. She tapped somewhere outside the brackets while there was still an operation inside them. This is usually reading left to right out of habit rather than a misunderstanding. It fades fast.
  • Multiplication outranks addition. She tapped a plus or a minus while a times, a divide, or an exponent was still waiting. This is the real error, not a slip. It means she is scanning for the leftmost operation instead of the highest ranked one. Slow down and name the rank out loud before every tap for a while.
  • Same rank. Work left to right. She tapped the right hand one of a pair that share a rank, so a times before a divide, or a plus before a minus. This is the mistake that PEMDAS itself causes, because the letters look like six ranked steps and two of those pairs are actually ties. If she is hitting this one, the fix is the reference card on this page, not more problems.
  • Exponents before multiplying. She tapped a times or a divide while a power was still unresolved. Often she just did not see the little number. Point at it rather than explaining anything.

Watch which of the four she repeats. One category showing up over and over is a rule she has not got yet; one of each scattered around is ordinary carelessness and will settle on its own.

Why exponents came first

Yesterday's page was powers, and that was on purpose. Exponents are rank 2 here, so every expression on this page needs her to be able to look at 4² and know it is 16 without stopping to think. If she is still working out powers by counting copies, this page turns into two hard things at once. Go back and run the speed round for a minute before starting, and it will not.

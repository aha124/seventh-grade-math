Move the Point

Multiplying decimals, one slide at a time

A decimal problem is a whole-number problem plus some bookkeeping. Slide each point to the right until both numbers are whole. Every slide is a ×10 you owe. Multiply the whole numbers, then pay the slides back: one ÷10 for each, sliding the point left through the answer. Do the four problems below in order.

To repay: ÷10 × 0
The long way

If you get stuck: look at the repay counter. Every ×10 you tapped is one ÷10 you still owe. When it reads 0, the point is home and you are done.

Before every multiply: estimate

Round each number to a whole number and multiply those in your head. Then do the real problem. If your real answer is not near your estimate, the point is in the wrong place. That is the whole check, and it takes five seconds.

3.6 × 1.2: 3.6 is about 4, 1.2 is about 1, and 4 × 1 = 4. So the answer is about 4. The digits are 432 either way; the estimate tells you where the point goes.

Which one is 3.6 × 1.2?

Do this on paper too, before the worksheet problems. Write the estimate in the margin, then multiply, then compare.

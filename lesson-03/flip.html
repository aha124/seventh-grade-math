The Flip, Proven

Why keep, change, flip works

You already know the move: keep the first, flip the second, multiply. This page is the reason it works, in three short acts. Nothing here is graded.

Teacher notes

Why this page exists

Keep, change, flip is easy to remember and easy to distrust. A rule that feels like magic gets second-guessed on a test, and the first "but why does that work?" from a tutor or a test question should not be the first time she hears the answer. This page makes the flip a consequence of two things she already accepts: a number times its reciprocal is 1, and multiplying top and bottom by the same thing changes nothing.

Run it even if she flips confidently

Confidence with the move is not the same as owning the reason. Ten minutes here, once, and the flip is hers to explain rather than recall. The three acts are the book's own argument in order: reciprocals make 1; a division is a fraction over a fraction, and multiplying both halves by the bottom's reciprocal turns the bottom into 1; what is left is the flipped multiplication.

The one line to say out loud

At the end of act 2: nothing was changed. Top and bottom were multiplied by the same thing, so the fraction is the same fraction. The flip is not a trick that gets applied; it is what remains after the denominator erases itself.

Act 3

Let her pick wrong. Multiplying by 2/3 makes the bottom 4/9, not 1, and the page says so; multiplying by the top's reciprocal erases the top instead of the bottom. Both wrong picks teach the thing the right pick cannot: only the bottom's reciprocal does the job.
